## About the Author

Steve Waldo was 22 at the time of publication and lived in Baraboo, Wisconsin. He was studying computer science (minor in fine art) at the University of Wisconsin at LaCrosse.

He got into computers in junior high school, where he and his friends played games heavily. When the school removed all games from its computer center, he began down the path that led to Wings of Fury. Playing games had made him fascinated by graphics and animation, which motivated him to learn programming properly; he found that creating animation was as much fun as playing games. Outside computing he enjoys outdoor sports such as skiing, and he is a keen student of World War II aircraft.

## Page 1–2: Intro: "A Time of Fury"

> [IMAGE: Winged badge logo with "WINGS" in a box above a black "OF FURY" bar, centered above the heading.]

### Story setup (summary)

- **Year:** 1944, during World War II.
- **Theater:** the Pacific. The Allies are gaining the upper hand, and the enemy is fighting hard to hold the territory it captured earlier in the war.
- The U.S. Navy's air arm is a decisive factor, and the standout aircraft is the **F6F Hellcat**: powerful, durable, able to carry **bombs, rockets and torpedoes**, more maneuverable than the enemy's best fighters, and holder of one of the best kill-to-loss ratios of the war.
- **Player role:** fly a Hellcat providing air cover for the aircraft carrier **USS Wasp**, which has been badly damaged and must get back to its home port.
- **Player tasks along the way:**
  1. Defend the carrier from **torpedo bombers**.
  2. Clear the enemy out of their **island strongholds**.
  3. **Sink enemy ships** on the route home.
  4. Survive by **shooting down enemy planes**.
- **Opening scene:** the signal officer clears the player for launch, the Hellcat takes off at full throttle, and shortly after leaving the deck the player spots **two enemy fighters diving in from above**.

(Page 2 also shows the Hellcat corner silhouette.)

## Page 3: "CONTACT!"

> [IMAGE: Winged section badge labeled "CONTACT!".]

### What you need

- An Apple IIe, IIc or IIGS **with a joystick**.
- An Apple IIe must have an **extended 80-column card**.
- A **color monitor** is recommended.

### Loading the game

1. Insert the Wings of Fury disk into **drive #1**, label side up.
2. Make sure the joystick is connected, then power on the computer and monitor.
3. The **title screen** appears, followed by a **self-running demo** (attract mode).
4. Press **any key or a joystick button** to leave the demo.
5. The game asks you to **flip the disk over** (the game is on a two-sided disk).
6. After flipping, press **any key or a joystick button** to start.

## Page 5: "PREFLIGHT CHECKLIST"

> [IMAGE: Winged section badge labeled "PREFLIGHT CHECKLIST".]

### Selecting a rank

- After leaving the demo, the first step is to **choose a Naval rank**.
- There are **7 ranks**; each one gives a harder set of missions than the previous one.
- Cycle through ranks with the **arrow keys or the joystick**; confirm with **Return or a joystick button**.
- After choosing, a **mission briefing message** lists the objectives for that mission.

### Selecting weapons

- Before takeoff, choose the Hellcat's **weapon load** for this flight.
- Cycle the weapons list with the **arrow keys or joystick**; confirm with **Return or a joystick button**.
- (See "Hellcat Weapons" below: the selectable secondary weapons are **Bombs**, **Rockets**, **Torpedo**. Machine guns are always available.)

### Taking off

1. Push the joystick **fully left** to apply **full throttle to the left** (the plane launches leftward along the deck).
2. As the plane approaches the **end of the flight deck**, push the stick **up-left** to climb.

## Page 6: Flying, turning, landing

### Flying: joystick map

The Hellcat is flown with an 8-way joystick (plus center). The manual shows this diagram:

> [IMAGE: Joystick-position diagram. A central rectangle labeled "3/4 Throttle" with eight lines radiating to labels. Layout as a 3×3 grid:]

```
   Left Climb        Stall         Right Climb
              \        |        /
Full Throttle Left — [3/4 Throttle] — Full Throttle Right
              /        |        \
   Left Dive       Stall Dive       Right Dive
```

| Stick position | Effect |
|---|---|
| Centered (neutral) | 3/4 throttle |
| Up | Stall |
| Down | Stall dive |
| Left | Full throttle, heading left |
| Right | Full throttle, heading right |
| Up-left | Climb, heading left |
| Up-right | Climb, heading right |
| Down-left | Dive, heading left |
| Down-right | Dive, heading right |

Note: Control-F (see Special Keys) inverts the vertical axis, so "up" and "down" above can be swapped by the player.

### Turning

- Turn around by **reversing the stick's horizontal direction** (left ↔ right).
- The plane **loses altitude while turning**, so the player should add lift (climb input) during the turn.

### Landing

1. Approach the carrier **flying into the wind, which comes from the right** (so the approach is made from the right side of the carrier).
2. Use the **3-D view** to line up the **flashing white artificial horizon indicator** with the carrier deck.
3. Right as the plane passes over the **edge of the deck**, **stall** the plane so it drops and its **arrester hook** catches one of the **4 arresting cables** on the deck.

## Page 7: Landing (cont.) and Hellcat Weapons

### Landing (continued)

- Carrier landings take skill; the manual suggests practicing them, because later the player will have to land **under enemy fire**.
- After landing, **taxi to the elevator** and press **a joystick button** to activate it. The elevator **lowers the plane into the hold** for **repairs, refueling and re-arming** (new weapon load).
- If the plane overshoots the elevator, **turn around** and position it over the elevator again.

### Hellcat weapons

| Weapon | Quantity per load | Fire control | Intended targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machine guns (.50 caliber) | 3 per wing (6 total); ammo count not stated | **Button 0**, in flight | Enemy planes, strafing islands |
| Bombs (100 lb) | 30 | **Button 1**, in flight (when Bombs selected) | Barracks, machine-gun dug-outs on islands |
| Rockets (5 inch) | 15 | **Button 1**, in flight (when Rockets selected) | Heavier targets such as large antiaircraft guns |
| Torpedo | Count not stated (counter in the panel image suggests the selected weapon's count is shown) | **Button 1**, in flight (when Torpedo selected) | Enemy ships, after their guns are disabled |

"Button 0" and "button 1" are the two Apple II joystick buttons. Only one secondary weapon type (bombs, rockets or torpedo) is carried per sortie, chosen on the Weapons menu.

## Page 8: Torpedoes and Control Panel

### Torpedoes

- A torpedo run is only possible **once the enemy ship's guns have been disabled**.
- Select **Torpedo** from the Weapons menu; release with **button 1**.
- Rules for release:
  - Approach the ship **flying low over the water**.
  - Make sure the ship is **well within sight** before releasing; the torpedo has **limited energy/range** and will stop running if released too far away.

### Control panel (HUD)

## Page 9: Control panel (cont.)

#### Hellcat counter

- Shows the number of **Hellcats (lives) remaining**.
- The player gets **one extra plane for every promotion** in rank.

#### Oil pressure gauge

- Represents **engine condition / damage**.
- When hit by gunfire, the engine **starts smoking**; **more damage → more smoke and lower oil pressure**.
- At a **critically low** level, a **red light blinks** on the gauge.
- If oil pressure falls **too low, the engine seizes and the plane crashes**.
- Descending into the carrier's hold **repairs the plane** and restores oil pressure **to full**.

#### 3-D view

- A **first-person, three-dimensional view from the cockpit**, mainly used to judge the **distance to an approaching ship, plane or other target**.
- In its center is a **flashing white cursor** that acts as an **artificial horizon indicator**, used for **aiming at targets** and **lining up carrier landings**.
- (From page 12) When a **torpedo plane** is spotted, an **arrow** appears in this view pointing toward its position relative to the player.

#### Fuel gauge

- Shows **remaining fuel**.
- At a **critically low** level, a **red light blinks** on the gauge.
- **Running out of fuel in flight causes a crash**.
- Descending into the carrier's hold **refuels the plane to full**.

## Page 10: Control panel (cont.)

#### Score counter

Shows all points the player has scored in the game.

#### Enemy plane counter

- Adds a **"kill" icon** for each enemy plane shot down (the small plane icons under the score).
- There is also a **numeric kill counter** below the score counter.

## Page 11: "FLYING THE MISSIONS"

> [IMAGE: Winged section badge labeled "FLYING THE MISSIONS".]

- The game starts with **3 Hellcats** (lives).
- **+1 Hellcat** for each promotion in rank.

### Mission objectives

- Each mission's objectives are given in a **briefing before it starts**.
- A **bonus** is awarded for **each objective** completed, plus an **additional bonus** for completing the **whole mission**.

The two main objective types:

#### Islands

- Targets: **barracks, soldiers, machine-gun dug-outs, large antiaircraft guns**.
- An island is **neutralized** only when **all barracks, soldiers and guns are destroyed**.

#### Ships

- An enemy ship is neutralized only when it is **sunk**.

Encountered in some missions:

#### Fighter planes

- Shooting down every fighter in an area is **not required** to complete a mission.

---

## Page 12: Torpedo planes and high scores

#### Torpedo planes

- To protect the carrier, the player must either **shoot down torpedo planes** or **destroy the torpedoes after they are dropped** (with **bullets, bombs or rockets**).
- When a torpedo plane is sighted, an **arrow appears in the 3-D view** showing where it is relative to the player.

### High scores

- At game end, if the score is in the **top ten**, it can be **saved to disk**.
- The player is asked to **enter a name**; the **rank is recorded automatically**.

## Appendix: Implementation quick reference (derived from the manual)

Everything here comes from the manual above; nothing is added from outside sources. Items the manual leaves unspecified are marked **unspecified**.

### Game constants

| Constant | Value |
|---|---|
| Starting lives | 3 |
| Extra life | +1 per rank promotion |
| Number of ranks | 7 (each harder than the last; rank names **unspecified**) |
| Bomb load | 30 × 100 lb |
| Rocket load | 15 × 5 inch |
| Torpedo load | **unspecified** |
| Machine guns | 6 × .50 cal (3 per wing); ammo **unspecified** (likely unlimited) |
| Arresting cables on carrier deck | 4 |
| High-score table size | 10 entries (name + score + rank) |
| Carrier | USS Wasp, damaged, heading to home port |
| Year / setting | 1944, Pacific |

### Input mapping

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Joystick 8-way + center | See flight table (page 6) |
| Joystick button 0 | Fire machine guns |
| Joystick button 1 | Release/fire selected secondary weapon |
| Joystick button (which one **unspecified**) on elevator | Lower plane into hold |
| Arrow keys / joystick + Return / button | Menu navigation (rank, weapons) |
| Esc, Ctrl-R, Ctrl-S, Ctrl-D, Ctrl-C, Ctrl-F | See Special Keys table |

### Game flow (state machine)

```
TITLE -> DEMO (attract) --any key/button--> FLIP_DISK_PROMPT --any key/button-->
RANK_SELECT -> MISSION_BRIEFING -> WEAPON_SELECT -> ON_DECK (takeoff leftward)
-> IN_FLIGHT
     -> LANDED (hook catches 1 of 4 cables) -> TAXI -> ELEVATOR (button)
        -> HOLD: repair (oil = full), refuel (fuel = full), WEAPON_SELECT -> ON_DECK
     -> CRASH (oil too low / fuel empty / other) -> lose a life
        -> lives > 0 ? next plane : GAME_OVER
     -> all mission objectives done -> mission bonus -> promotion (+1 life) -> next mission
GAME_OVER -> HIGH_SCORE_ENTRY (if top 10) -> RANK_SELECT
Ctrl-R anywhere -> RANK_SELECT
```

### Key mechanics

- **View:** side-scrolling, with the plane flying left or right; plus a small first-person 3-D view window in the HUD.
- **Throttle model:** neutral stick = 3/4 throttle; horizontal deflection = full throttle in that direction.
- **Turning** reverses heading and costs altitude.
- **Stall** (stick up) makes the plane drop; used deliberately for landing.
- **Damage model:** damage lowers oil pressure and adds engine smoke (more damage = more smoke); warning light when critical; engine seizure = crash.
- **Fuel:** depletes over time; warning light when critical; empty = crash.
- **Torpedo:** only usable against a ship whose guns are disabled; must be released low over the water and close enough, because the torpedo runs out of energy.
- **Enemy torpedo planes** threaten the carrier; counter by shooting them down or destroying their dropped torpedoes with guns, bombs or rockets; an arrow in the 3-D view shows their direction.
- **Island neutralization:** destroy all barracks, soldiers, machine-gun dug-outs and AA guns.
- **Ship neutralization:** sink it.
- **Fighters:** optional to kill.
- **Scoring:** points per target (values **unspecified**), bonus per objective, bonus per completed mission.
